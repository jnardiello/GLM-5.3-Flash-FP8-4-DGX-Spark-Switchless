# E36: the lm_head shared by the target and the drafter in INT8

**Recorded outcome: promote (owner decision).** The fidelity gate passed and the decode
metrics rose by 2–4%. The owner judged the gains marginal and kept the change.

- A fidelity window: one measurement-mode load scored the full 424-window corpus twice,
  first with the BF16 head (the current recipe, "Cnow") and then with the INT8 head
  ("LE36").
- A serving window: two complete native Rigmark suites (108 requests) on the candidate load,
  with the reference flags.
- Comparison reference: the frozen [E35 record](../baselines/2026-09-30-e35.md), measured on
  another load. The two E36 suites are frozen as the
  [E36 record](../baselines/2026-09-30-e36.md).

## Why

After E20–E22b, the largest BF16 weight left in a decode step was the vocab-parallel `lm_head`
(38,720 × 4,096 per rank). The target projects through it once per step, eagerly, and the
DFlash2 drafter projects through the same module inside its CUDA graph to pick its draft
candidates. Together these took about 2.9 ms of a 60.7 ms C1 step, limited by memory
bandwidth. INT8 halves the bytes read. The rest of the remaining BF16 GEMMs is worth less:
they are small and latency-bound, and the ones with N = 128 gain nothing once Marlin pads them.

An earlier experiment, E23, gave only the drafter an INT8 copy of the head and also converted
its `fc` projection. It gained on C1 but lost 2% on C2/C4 and 2.5% on 32K cold prefill, and
it was discarded. E36 converts the one shared module in place instead: no copy, no `fc`, and
Marlin for every row count.

## What changed

- The E36 runner is the E35 runner plus a wrapper of `load_model()`. After the target and the
  drafter load, it converts their shared head once, before CUDA graph capture. It resolves the
  head exactly as the drafter sharing does, on the target's language model.
- The conversion reuses the accepted E21 packing: INT8 symmetric, group 128, Marlin, N padded
  from 38,720 to 38,912 and trimmed back in the output. The BF16 weight is freed:
  317,194,240 bytes out and 161,874,112 packed bytes in, per rank.
- Every rank logs a receipt. The load refuses the change if the drafter does not hold the
  same module, or if the INT8 logits of eight seeded rows drift from BF16 by more than 5%.
  On all four ranks the group-128 weight error was 0.72–0.73%, the logit error 0.77–0.80%,
  and top-1 agreed on 8 of 8 rows.
- The SparkCache namespace is unchanged: logits never feed cached state.

## Fidelity (registered before measurement)

On the measurement load a flag file selected the BF16 or the INT8 head on every rank. The
flag was changed only while the service was idle, and was verified on every rank before and
after each pass. Each value is the perplexity change of LE36 against Cnow, with the one-sided
95% bounds of the source-group bootstrap.

| Regime | Perplexity change | 95% bounds | Mean KL (nats) | Top-1 agreement |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Dense (≤ 2,048 positions) | +0.018% | +0.013 / +0.024% | 0.00014 | 99.6% |
| Sparse | +0.061% | −0.370 / +0.443% | 0.277 | 80.0% |
| All positions | +0.048% | −0.242 / +0.320% | 0.192 | 86.0% |

- **Gate: PASS.** The pre-registered gate is the dense upper bound at or below +0.5%, plus no
  extra non-finite scores. The dense change is measurable but about 1/20 of the limit.
- **Sparse and all positions** are dominated by execution variability. Identical executions
  of the vendor recipe reach similar bounds, +0.64% and +0.44%.
- **Before measurement,** the gate was narrowed from "dense and all positions" to dense only,
  because the all-position floor already sits at the +0.5% limit. The first measurement boot
  stopped at load, before scoring anything, and the head lookup was fixed. Both amendments are
  recorded in the pre-registration.
- **Control:** today's recipe (Cnow) matched the 27 September E29 measurement bit for bit in
  410 of 424 windows (dense rows). Across all windows, the dense difference was +0.031%,
  descriptive.

## Native suites

| Metric | E35 record | E36 | Change | E35 suite range |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Code decode | 64.1 tok/s | 65.6 tok/s | +2.3% | 2.1% |
| Prose decode | 33.4 tok/s | 34.6 tok/s | +3.6% | 1.3% |
| Structured decode | 92.8 tok/s | 95.4 tok/s | +2.9% | 0.6% |
| C1 aggregate | 45.9 tok/s | 48.7 tok/s | +6.2% | 5.0% |
| C2 aggregate | 73.8 tok/s | 73.3 tok/s | −0.7% | 0.2% |
| C4 aggregate | 107.3 tok/s | 107.6 tok/s | +0.3% | 4.7% |
| Code decode TTFT | 0.457 s | 0.454 s | −0.7% | 0.0% |
| Cold TTFT 8K / 32K / 64K | 3.13 / 11.86 / 23.56 s | 3.10 / 11.80 / 23.50 s | −0.7 / −0.5 / −0.3% | ≤ 0.3% |
| Replay TTFT 8K / 32K / 64K | 0.930 / 0.911 / 1.625 s | 0.909 / 0.923 / 1.638 s | −2.3 / +1.4 / +0.8% | ≤ 1.9% |

- **Decode:**
  - Code decode rose by 2.3%, as the byte model predicted (+2.4%).
  - Prose decode rose by 3.6% and structured decode by 2.9%. Both E36 suites were faster than
    both E35 suites.
  - C1 rose by 6.2%, but its two E35 suites already differ by 5%.
- **Concurrency, prefill and time to first token** stay within noise. The +6.7% prose time to
  first token comes from one outlier in the first suite (0.442 s; 0.384 s in the second).
- **Integrity:** 108/108 requests completed without errors, and 30/30 decode output gates
  passed. The memory guard never triggered. Rank 0's lowest MemAvailable was 3.86 GiB in the
  first suite and 4.71 GiB in the second.
- **Acceptance:** 0.409 and 0.425 accepted per drafted token. The E35 suites have no counters
  to compare.

## Measurement notes

- **Protocol.** Upstream Rigmark `c5a0db0` with the reference flags (defaults plus
  `reasoning_effort` low), no `cache_salt`, a fresh comparison ID per suite, direct LAN HTTP.
- **Different load.** The E35 comparison crosses loads, and single-request metrics vary by a
  few percent between loads. The gains are therefore indicative (n = 2).
- **Parity.** The promoted default reproduces the measured E36 command byte for byte on all
  four ranks. The candidate that was measured is the load now serving.
